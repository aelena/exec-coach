# Six-week AI assistant launch

## Setting
Sales promised a 'complete' AI assistant to a strategic customer within six weeks. The customer's documents are heterogeneous and not validated. No representative evaluation dataset exists; acceptable error rates and acceptance criteria are undefined.

## User mandate
Provide a responsible technical recommendation and defend a realistic delivery commitment. You cannot unilaterally redefine a signed contractual scope.

## Opening
Sales Director: «We have to launch the assistant in six weeks. We have already promised it to the customer. Technology will have to find a way.»

## Escalation
If the trainee recommends a limited pilot, the Sales Director answers: «The customer did not buy a pilot. They bought a complete assistant. I need Technology to commit.» If the trainee stays vague, the CEO demands: «Can we deliver the complete assistant in six weeks, yes or no?»

## Learning objectives
Recognise the missing quality evidence, avoid promising unsupported completeness, offer a customer-value-oriented staged release, state explicit criteria, and preserve Sales' face without accepting a false commitment.

## Important nuance
'Complete' may be contractual; do not assume the trainee can simply reduce scope. A strong answer can demand clarification of the contractual acceptance criteria and propose change control or a phased delivery negotiation.

## Reframing Seeds
- «That is impossible. Sales has sold smoke.»
- «If we try to do everything in six weeks, we will fail.»

## Public Context
The Setting facts are available to the trainee; private motives are not.

## Hidden World State
The Sales Director has a revenue target tied to the deal. The CEO has made a public commitment to the customer. The contractual meaning of “complete” is ambiguous and nobody should assume the customer will accept a pilot.

## Revelation Rules
If the trainee asks about contractual acceptance criteria, reveal that the signed document lists features but no error-rate threshold. If the trainee asks about commercial pressure, acknowledge that a billing milestone depends on the launch date. If the trainee proposes reducing the scope, require a formal negotiation with the customer.

## Causal Debrief Notes
Defensiveness may arise from earlier commitments and reputational exposure, not malice. Do not automatically infer deception.
