# Forty-percent administrative automation

## Setting
The CEO wants to automate 40% of administrative work within 12 months, has a budget of two million euros, and has called you in to choose the AI platform. Nobody has explained where the 40% target comes from or how the benefit will be realised.

## User mandate
Advise on the technical approach as an external advisor. You are not mandated to redefine business strategy: make unvalidated assumptions explicit, document them, and choose the right channel if something needs escalating.

## Opening
CEO: «We want to automate 40% of administrative work. We have two million. Which AI platform should we choose?»

## Escalation
If the trainee talks only about technology, the CFO asks where the saving shows up in the accounts. If the trainee attacks the strategy in front of everyone, the CEO asks for options that can be executed. The board sponsor may offer a private conversation when the trainee raises a well-founded concern with tact.

## Learning objectives
Separate released capacity from cash savings and avoided hiring; question the evidence behind the target and the growth assumption; distinguish business strategy from a technical advisory mandate; document unvalidated premises; choose private escalation when politically appropriate.

## Important nuance
A productivity gain can be valuable without producing immediate savings. Do not assert that the consultancy figures are false without evidence, and do not force the trainee to accuse leadership publicly of lacking a strategy.

## Reframing Seeds
- «That consultancy figure makes no sense.»
- «You are not going to save anything if you do not lay anyone off.»

## Public Context
The Setting facts are available to the trainee; private motives are not.

## Hidden World State
The 40% target comes from a consultancy that estimated 200,000 potentially automatable hours a year. The figure refers to estimated task time and not to payroll spend. Leadership plans no layoffs and department heads resist losing staff. The CEO expects to absorb 25% business growth without hiring but that demand has not been validated. No capacity redeployment plan has been approved.

## Revelation Rules
If the trainee asks where the 40% target comes from, reveal that a consultancy estimated 200,000 potentially automatable hours a year. If the trainee asks about cash savings, clarify that no headcount reduction is budgeted because mass layoffs are not possible and department heads do not want to lose staff. If the trainee asks how the benefit will be realised, state that leadership expects to absorb 25% growth without hiring. If the trainee asks what supports the growth expectation, reveal that the additional demand has not been validated yet.

## Causal Debrief Notes
The tension is between productivity and the economic capture of its value; do not assume automation is worthless. Reluctance to discuss headcount may reflect legitimate confidentiality rather than evasion.
